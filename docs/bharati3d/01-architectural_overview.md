# Bharati Station: Architectural & Structural Overview

**Architect:** bof architekten (Bücking, Ostrop & Flemming) and IMS Ingenieurgesellschaft  
**Location:** Larsemann Hills, Antarctica (69°24′S 76°11′E)  
**Commissioned By:** National Centre for Polar and Ocean Research (NCPOR), India  
**Operational Since:** March 2012  

## 1. Core Architectural Concept
Bharati is a highly distinct, modern, and modular facility designed specifically to withstand the extreme environment of Antarctica (temperatures down to -40°C and winds exceeding 300 km/h). The facility was designed with a strict mandate: minimize the environmental footprint and ensure it can be entirely dismantled and removed in the future, complying with the Antarctic Treaty.

## 2. Modular Container Construction
The underlying structural skeleton of the station is entirely modular. 
*   **134 Prefabricated Shipping Containers:** Instead of traditional steel framing built on-site, the building's core utilizes 134 standard shipping containers. 
*   **Logistical Efficiency:** This allowed the station to be prefabricated in Germany, shipped to Antarctica, and assembled rapidly during the short Antarctic summer window.

## 3. Aerodynamic Insulated Skin
The container assembly is not exposed to the elements. The entire modular core is wrapped in a specialized aerodynamic exterior skin.
*   **Material:** Thermally insulated aluminum panels.
*   **Shape:** The distinctive curved, aerodynamic shape was meticulously wind-tunnel tested to ensure that high-velocity blizzard winds flow smoothly over the structure. This prevents massive snowdrifts from burying the facility over time.

## 4. Elevated Foundation (Stilts)
*   **Stilt Columns:** The entire building is elevated above the ground on heavy-duty steel columns/stilts.
*   **Purpose:** Elevating the structure serves two critical functions:
    1.  It allows snow and wind to blow *underneath* the station, virtually eliminating snow accumulation against the building.
    2.  It insulates the underlying Antarctic permafrost from the heat generated by the station, preventing the ice from melting and destabilizing the foundation.

## 5. Honors & Recognition
The innovative design has been recognized globally, winning the **European Steel Design Award (2013)** and the **Ingenieurpreis des Deutschen Stahlbaus (2013)**.
