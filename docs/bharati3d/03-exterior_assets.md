# Bharati Station: Exterior Assets & Environment

To construct an accurate 3D model, the external environment and scattered assets around the main building must be represented.

## 1. Fuel Farm & Infrastructure
*   **Fuel Storage Capacity:** The station boasts a highly automated fuel farm with a capacity of roughly **300,000 liters (3 lakh liters)**.
*   **Fuel Type:** Jet A-1 / Special Kerosene is primarily used because it remains liquid at extreme polar temperatures (down to -54°C).
*   **Tanks:** These are typically arrayed in interconnected cylindrical tanks mounted securely near the main structure, piping directly into the Ground Floor CHP (Combined Heat and Power) units.

## 2. Bharati Heliport
*   **Coordinates:** ~69°24′24″S 76°11′36″E.
*   **Surface:** Built on concrete/solid platforms.
*   **Operations:** Used heavily during the Antarctic summer for cargo resupply via heavy-lift helicopters (e.g., Kamov models) deployed from icebreaker ships.

## 3. Communication & Meteorological Systems
*   **SATCOM Domes:** C-band and Ku-band satellite radomes are typically mounted on or near the roof terrace to maintain continuous uplink with NCPOR Headquarters in Goa, India.
*   **Weather Masts:** Anemometers, barometers, and temperature gauges mounted on tall masts above the boundary layer of the station's aerodynamic shell to record accurate ambient weather data.

## 4. Surrounding Terrain (Larsemann Hills)
*   The station is located in the Larsemann Hills area, situated between the polar ice cap and the sea.
*   The ground is characterized by rocky, ice-free coastal hills interspersed with permafrost and snowdrifts. 
*   **Proximity:** Situated close to Quilty Bay, allowing for a seawater intake pipeline system (often trace-heated to prevent freezing) that supplies the station's needs.
